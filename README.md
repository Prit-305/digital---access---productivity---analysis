# Digital Access vs Economic Productivity Analysis

## Project Overview
This project explores the relationship between internet access and economic productivity across 60 countries using World Bank data.

## Research Question
Does greater digital access correspond to higher economic productivity?

## Objectives
- Examine internet access across countries.
- Compare economic productivity across countries.
- Explore differences across income groups.
- Identify potential implications for digital inclusion policies.

## Data Source
World Bank — World Development Indicators (WDI).

## Tools
- Microsoft Excel
- Data visualization

## Methodology
- Data collection
- Data cleaning
- Exploratory data analysis
- Cross-country comparison
- Interpretation of findings

## Key Findings
High internet penetration is correlated with higher average productivity, as the dataset shows a clear upward progression across internet usage tiers.
1. Low Internet Access Acts as a Floor on Productivity
In the scatter plot, every country with less than 40% internet adoption remains tightly clustered at the bottom of the chart with a GDP per person employed under $20,000 (such as Burundi, Chad, Uganda, Mozambique, Ethiopia, Afghanistan, and Niger). The data shows no instance of a country achieving high productivity without first achieving significant internet penetration.
2. High Internet Access Does Not Guarantee High Productivity
The data shows widespread variance in productivity among countries with similar high internet access rates:
Luxembourg / Ireland vs. Vietnam:  Luxembourg has internet penetration above 95% with a GDP per worker of ~$263,700, and Ireland reaches ~$234,300, whereas Vietnam has an internet penetration of 84.15% with a GDP per worker of $26,057—showing nearly a 10-fold difference despite comparable high digital coverage.
China vs. Brazil vs. Mexico vs. Emerging Tiers:  China (92.00% internet, $45,834 GDP/worker), Brazil (84.46% internet, $41,441 GDP/worker), Mexico (83.12% internet, $48,518 GDP/worker), and Thailand (~85% internet, ~$35,000 GDP/worker) have achieved digital reach similar to advanced economies, yet their productivity remains in the mid-tier.
United States & Advanced Economies vs. Russia:  The United States stands at $153,969 GDP per worker with 94.69% internet penetration, significantly outperforming countries like France ($127,332), Germany ($123,669), and the UK ($109,200), while Russia sits at $85,392 despite having high internet adoption (94.36%).
Resource & Specialized Hub Outliers:  High capital and resource-heavy economies like Guyana ($204,800 GDP/worker at ~78% internet), Macao SAR ($194,100), Norway ($178,800), and Brunei Darussalam ($163,200) achieve extreme productivity outcomes driven by sector-specific dynamics rather than digital infrastructure alone.
 India and its neighbours:   India appears in the lower-middle portion of the graph: its internet penetration is substantially higher than many poorer countries, but GDP per person employed remains far below the high-income cluster.
India has moved relatively far along the digital-access dimension, but has not moved an equivalent distance along the productivity dimension. In other words, India's position suggests that having a large share of the population connected to the internet does not automatically result in high productivity per employed person.
India has higher internet penetration than Bangladesh, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka in the 2024 data.
India is well below China and Bhutan in internet penetration.
India's GDP per person employed is above Bangladesh, Nepal, Pakistan and Myanmar, but below Bhutan, Sri Lanka and China.
Within the current lower-middle-income group, India's 2024 internet penetration (~65%) is above the group average (~55%), and its productivity (~$23,430) is also above the group's average (~$21,167).

## Limitations
Correlation does not establish causation.

## Conclusion
The project aims to explore how digital access relates to economic productivity and inform evidence-based policy discussions.
